Slate 2026.10: Notes and Forge come together

By Iian Kehn

Slate 2026.10 brings Slate Notes and Slate Forge into one Android writing app. Quick notes and fully laid-out documents now share one library and one editor.

Start with a note. Build a document.

A new document starts as a lightweight note. Add page-layout features and Slate preserves the full Forge document model automatically. The distinction follows the document’s structure, so a long note stays a note and a short letter can use Forge’s layout tools.

Rich text, headings, quotes, links, lists, checklists, tables, and images sit alongside undo, redo, and keyboard shortcuts. Forge tools add page setup, sections, margins, orientation, columns, headers, footers, page breaks, and paginated editing.

A calmer workspace, on every screen

The redesigned start center puts creation, templates, and your library within reach, with search, filters, favorites, archive, and trash. Slate’s cat-fox artwork gives it a clearer identity without crowding the page.

Android’s light and dark themes carry through to the document canvas. On phones, a collapsible formatting ribbon and compact landscape layout leave more room to write above the keyboard. Tablet and large-window layouts include foundations for keyboard, mouse, and Googlebook Android use.

Files you can take with you

Import .slx, .slxf, TXT, Markdown, and DOCX files. Export to those formats or PDF, then use Android’s share and print tools. The .slx format carries rich notes; .slxf keeps Forge’s complete page and layout model.

Exporting a Forge document as .slx can simplify page-specific features in the exported copy. Your original document stays intact. Advanced DOCX features can also simplify during conversion, and PDF output uses Slate’s own page rendering.

Your writing stays local

Slate requires no account and includes no advertising, analytics, telemetry, or background update polling. Documents stay in app-private storage until you choose to export, print, or share them. Automatic Android cloud backup and broad storage access are disabled, and screenshot and recent-app preview capture remain blocked while Slate is visible.

Updates are manual and use the official GitHub release, SHA-256 verification, and Android’s package-signature checks. Cloud sync and real-time collaboration are not part of this release.

Upgrading from Notes or standalone Forge

Existing Slate Notes users can install the official signed APK over their current installation; the local library migrates in place. Do not uninstall first, because uninstalling removes app-private documents.

The former standalone Slate Forge app has a different Android identity. Export its documents as .slxf or .slx, then import them into the unified Slate app.

Available now

Slate 2026.10 supports Android 12 or newer, including ARM64 and x86_64-compatible Android environments. Encrypted whole-library backup, guided standalone-Forge migration, and further large-document performance work remain planned.
